# Frank Energie billing explained

This document describes Frank Energie's billing method for Dutch dynamic electricity and gas contracts, based on the supplier's published explanation and invoice guide.

## Dynamic monthly billing

Frank Energie bills energy in advance. The regular monthly invoice is sent around the 11th, with collection around the 18th (an alternative collection date around the 25th can be requested).

The monthly invoice combines estimates for the upcoming month with a correction for a previous month:

1. **Upcoming month estimate:** Frank estimates the next month's consumption using the expected annual consumption and the connection's MFFBAS consumption profile (formerly NEDU profiles), rather than dividing annual consumption evenly by 12. It also estimates the prices for that month.
2. **Actual costs:** After a month ends, actual electricity consumption and hourly prices, and gas consumption and daily prices, are known.
3. **Correction:** The difference between the previous month's estimated and actual energy costs is settled on the next monthly invoice.

### Example of the monthly cycle

An invoice received in April may include:
- Fixed delivery charges, network charges and energy-tax instalments.
- Estimated consumption costs for May.
- A correction for actual consumption and prices in March, compared with the amount previously estimated and paid for March.

## Dynamic advance payment versus fixed advance payment

Frank Energie uses a dynamic advance payment rather than a fixed monthly advance amount.

### Dynamic advance payment

- The advance payment is recalculated every month, based on expected consumption for that month and estimated market prices.
- The expected consumption reflects the consumption profile and seasonal factors such as weather.
- Energy tax is spread across the year: normally one-twelfth of the estimated annual energy-tax amount is included each month.
- At the end of each month, the estimated costs are compared with the actual consumption and actual prices.
- The difference is settled on a subsequent monthly invoice: an under-estimate increases a later invoice, while an over-estimate reduces a later payment.

This means monthly payments typically rise in winter and fall in summer, reflecting seasonal consumption and price estimates rather than keeping the same payment amount all year.

### Fixed advance payment

With a fixed advance payment, the customer pays the same amount every month, regardless of the month's actual consumption or prices. The amount is estimated from expected annual consumption and prices. Summer overpayments may act as a buffer for winter consumption. The main reconciliation then takes place on the annual or final settlement.

### What is the practical difference?

Both methods can result in the same total cost for identical consumption and prices; they differ mainly in how payments are distributed through the year and when deviations are settled. Frank's dynamic method settles estimated-versus-actual energy costs monthly, reducing the size of the year-end correction for those components. A fixed advance payment postpones much of that reconciliation until the annual or final bill.

The annual or final settlement still applies with Frank's dynamic method. It remains relevant for reconciling annual components such as energy tax and other charges based on estimated annual consumption.

## Invoice components

### Fixed and regulated components

These are normally spread evenly over 12 monthly instalments:
- Fixed delivery charges.
- Energy taxes and the annual energy-tax reduction, based on the expected annual consumption and applicable rates.
- Network charges.

Energy taxes are calculated using estimated (net) annual consumption multiplied by the applicable tax rate, divided by 12. Network costs and other non-energy components are likewise included as monthly instalments. Differences from actual annual consumption or applicable charges are reconciled on the annual or final bill.

### Dynamic energy components

For electricity and gas:
- **Estimated costs:** estimated consumption and estimated average price for the upcoming month.
- **Actual costs:** actual consumption and the weighted average price for a previous month, reflecting the prices at the times of consumption.
- **Previously paid:** the estimate already billed for that previous month, against which actual costs are compared.

The monthly adjustment therefore primarily concerns the actual energy consumption and its applicable dynamic prices, rather than recalculating all fixed and regulated charges each month.

### Electricity export

The invoice may also show expected and actual proceeds for exported electricity. Actual export proceeds are based on the applicable market prices and export-specific surcharges or deductions. The invoice guide explains that the market price for export is the same underlying market price as consumption, but different additions or deductions may apply.

## Annual or final settlement

After a year of supply, or when the contract ends, Frank Energie issues an annual or final settlement, normally within six weeks.

The settlement compares the costs for the billing period with all invoiced instalments. Because actual electricity and gas consumption costs are already reconciled monthly, the largest remaining differences may be related to energy taxes and other instalments based on estimated annual consumption.

- If actual costs exceed paid instalments, the difference is collected, normally seven days after the settlement is received.
- If paid instalments exceed actual costs, the excess is refunded or may be partly offset against the next instalment.

The settlement is a reconciliation of charges, not necessarily a payment-history statement.

## First billing month

If supply starts during the signup month, Frank may send an invoice for the remaining days of that month as well as the next month's regular instalment. If fewer than four supply days remain in that first month, those days are generally included in the annual or final settlement instead.

## Timing summary

| Item | Typical timing |
|---|---|
| Monthly invoice | Around the 11th |
| Collection | Around the 18th |
| Alternative collection date | Around the 25th, by request |
| Upcoming month | Estimated and billed in advance |
| Previous month | Actual costs reconciled on the following invoice |
| Annual/final settlement | Within six weeks |

These dates are typical rather than guaranteed.
